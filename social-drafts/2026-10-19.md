Önce iyi haber: 2023'te yayımlanan EMIKETO çalışması, konuda ilk randomize kontrollü veriyi sunuyor. Fazla kilolu, sık migren yaşayan 57 kişi iki gruba ayrılmış — biri çok düşük kalorili ketojenik diyete, biri dengeli düşük kalorili bir diyete geçmiş. 8 hafta sonunda keto grubunda migren günleri ortalama 6,4 gün azalmış, diğer grupta 2,2 gün kalmış, fark istatistiksel olarak anlamlı (p=0,008). 12. haftada da fark sürmüş.

2024'te yayımlanan daha küçük bir pilot çalışma ise ilginç bir ayrım getiriyor: kronik migren hastalarında, kilo kaybı için henüz çok erken olan 4. haftada bile anlamlı iyileşme görülmüş. Bu, etkinin sadece kilo kaybından gelmediğine, ketozun kendisinin de bir payı olabileceğine işaret ediyor — ama bu çalışmanın kontrol grubu yok, o yüzden temkinli okumak gerekiyor.

Asıl dikkatimi çeken kısım şu oldu: migren önleyici olarak sık kullanılan topiramat, idrar kimyasını değiştiren bir ilaç ve kendi başına metabolik asidoza yol açabiliyor. Keto da benzer bir eğilim oluşturuyor. İlacın resmi bilgisinde bu ikisinin birlikte kullanılmasının böbrek taşı riskini artırabileceği, özellikle taş öyküsü olanlarda dikkatli olunması gerektiği yazıyor. Bu, popüler keto-migren içeriklerinin çoğunda hiç geçmeyen bir uyarı.

## Medium

**Title:** What a Randomized Trial Actually Found About Keto and Migraine
**Subtitle:** The evidence is real, early, and comes with a drug-interaction warning most articles skip

Migraine and diet have been linked in casual advice for years — cut this, avoid that — but most of it is anecdotal. What changed recently is that a randomized controlled trial finally looked at a ketogenic diet specifically, with measurable outcomes instead of testimonials.

**The trial: EMIKETO, 2023**

Published in the Journal of Translational Medicine, EMIKETO enrolled 57 people with high-frequency episodic migraine and a BMI above 27. Participants were randomized into two groups: one followed a very-low-calorie ketogenic diet (VLCKD), the other a balanced hypocaloric diet. At week 8, the keto group's migraine days dropped by an average of 6.4 days, compared to 2.2 days in the comparison group — a statistically significant difference (p=0.008) that held through week 12.

The keto group also lost more weight (8.2 kg versus 4.3 kg at week 8), and that's where the open question starts: obesity is independently linked to migraine, so how much of the improvement came from weight loss versus ketosis itself isn't fully separated in this design.

**A smaller study tries to answer that question**

A 2024 pilot study in Clinical Nutrition followed 25 people with chronic migraine on a Mediterranean-style ketogenic diet for 8 weeks. The notable part: a significant reduction in migraine frequency and intensity showed up by week 4 — too early for meaningful weight loss to explain it. That suggests ketosis itself might carry an independent effect. But this was an uncontrolled pilot, so it can't rule out a placebo or time effect, and the authors call for larger controlled trials.

**The part most articles leave out**

If you're on a migraine preventive like topiramate, there's a specific interaction worth knowing before trying keto. Topiramate is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor that can independently lower urinary citrate and cause metabolic acidosis. A ketogenic diet pushes in a similar direction. The drug's own prescribing information warns that combining topiramate with another agent that causes metabolic acidosis — a ketogenic diet included — can raise the risk of kidney stones, and recommends caution and good hydration, especially for anyone with a stone history.

**Where this leaves things**

Two small studies, one randomized and one not, point in a genuinely promising direction. Neither is strong enough to call keto a migraine treatment, and neither should be read as a reason to stop medication. What they do support is a more informed conversation with a doctor — one that includes the EMIKETO numbers and the topiramate caveat, not just "try cutting carbs."
