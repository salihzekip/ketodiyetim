**Konu başlığı:** Tiroidin varsa keto'ya girmeden önce bil bunları
**Önizleme satırı:** 1976'dan bir çalışma ile 2025'ten bir çalışma aynı soruya iki farklı açıdan cevap veriyor.

Bu hafta blogda yeni bir yazı var ve itiraf edeyim, araştırırken ben de biraz şaşırdım.

Keto'ya başlamak isteyen danışanlarımdan tiroid öyküsü olanlar hep aynı soruyu soruyor: "Karbonhidratı kesersem tiroidim bozulur mu?" Çoğu zaman net bir cevap vermek zor, çünkü literatür tam da bu yüzden ilginç — iki farklı dönemden iki çalışma var ve ikisi de haklı görünüyor.

Birincisi klasik bir çalışma, 1976'dan. Araştırmacılar insanları aç bırakmış ya da çok düşük karbonhidratlı, çok düşük kalorili diyetlere almış ve T3 hormonunun (tiroidin asıl iş gören hormonu) düştüğünü görmüşler — tam açlıkta yaklaşık %53, karbonhidratsız düşük kalorili diyette iki haftada yaklaşık %47. Ama ilginç olan şu: günde en az 50 gram karbonhidrat alındığında bu düşüş görülmemiş. Yani eşik, sandığımızdan daha belirgin.

İkincisi ise çok daha yeni, 2025'in ortasında yayımlanmış. Bu kez 40 Hashimoto hastası iki gruba ayrılmış: biri normal diyetine devam etmiş, biri düşük karbonhidrata geçmiş. Altı ay sonra düşük karbonhidrat grubunda tiroiddeki iltihap belirteci ve antikor seviyeleri (TPOAb, TgAb) düşmüş; normal diyet grubunda hiçbir şey değişmemiş.

Yani ortaya çıkan tablo şu: karbonhidratı çok sert kısarsan T3 üretimi baskılanabilir, ama orta düzeyde kısıtlama tiroiddeki iltihabı azaltabiliyor. Bu ikisi birbirini yalanlamıyor, sadece farklı mekanizmalardan bahsediyor — biri hormon üretimi, biri bağışıklık süreci.

Pratikte ne anlama geliyor bu? KetoDiyetim'in standart planı günde 20-25 gram net karbonhidrat öneriyor, yani o eşiğin altında. Tiroid öykün varsa bunu bilerek başlamak, TSH'ın yanında serbest T3'ü de ölçtürmek, ve yorgunluk gibi belirtileri "keto gribi" diye geçiştirmeden takip etmek önemli.

## Medium

**Title:** What Carb Restriction Actually Does to Your Thyroid
**Subtitle:** Two studies, fifty years apart, tell two different parts of the same story

"Can I do keto if I have a thyroid condition?" This question comes up constantly, and it comes up almost entirely from women — thyroid disorders are far more common in women than men, and there's a reasonable fear that cutting carbs will throw an already delicate hormonal system further off balance.

The honest answer has two parts, and most of what's written online only covers one of them.

**Part one: carbohydrate and T3 have a documented relationship, and it's older than you'd think.**

The thyroid produces T4, but T3 is the hormone that actually does the work in your cells, and the conversion from T4 to T3 is sensitive to how many carbs you're eating. A study from 1976, published in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, put participants through total fasting and through very-low-calorie diets with different carbohydrate levels. During complete fasting, T3 fell by about 53%. On a carbohydrate-free, 800-calorie diet, it fell by roughly 47% over two weeks. But diets that kept at least 50 grams of carbohydrate a day showed no significant change in T3 at all.

That's a meaningful detail, because it suggests a fairly specific threshold rather than a vague "low carb is risky" warning. Below roughly 50 grams a day, the body seems to interpret the restriction as a signal to conserve energy and dials down T3 production. Above that, it mostly doesn't.

Here's where it gets trickier: TSH, the hormone a standard thyroid panel usually checks, tends to stay in the normal range even while T3 is dropping. That means a "normal" thyroid panel can hide a real shift — unless free T3 is checked specifically.

**Part two: a 2025 study found the opposite effect on inflammation.**

Published in Medical Science Monitor, this trial randomized 40 patients with Hashimoto's thyroiditis into two groups — one kept their normal diet, one switched to a low-carbohydrate diet. After six months, researchers used MRI to measure water content in thyroid tissue, an indirect marker of inflammation. The low-carb group showed a significant drop in that marker, along with lower levels of two antibodies associated with Hashimoto's (TPOAb and TgAb). The group on a normal diet showed no change on any measure.

Read side by side, these two findings aren't a contradiction — they're describing two different systems. One is about hormone production adapting to available fuel. The other is about an autoimmune process that may respond to a different metabolic environment. A person can experience both at once: slightly lower T3, and also calmer thyroid-related inflammation.

**What this means in practice**

If you have a thyroid condition and you're considering a ketogenic diet (strict keto is typically 20-25 grams of net carbs a day, well under that ~50g threshold), a few things are worth doing before you start:

- Ask for free T3 and free T4 to be checked alongside TSH, not instead of it.
- If you're on levothyroxine or another thyroid medication, never adjust the dose yourself — weight change can alter how the drug is absorbed.
- If you notice ongoing fatigue or cold intolerance that doesn't fade after the first couple of weeks, consider raising net carbs into the 30-50 gram range rather than assuming it's just "keto flu."
- Get rechecked after 6-8 weeks so you're looking at real data, not guesswork.

Neither study supports a sweeping claim in either direction. The 1976 trial was done in people with obesity, under more extreme conditions than a typical ad libitum keto diet. The 2025 study was a single-center trial of 40 people. What both studies agree on is narrower and more useful than a slogan: carbohydrate intake measurably affects thyroid physiology, and the effect isn't the same for every part of that physiology.
