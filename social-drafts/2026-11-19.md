## Substack

**Konu başlığı:** Keto uykunuzu mu düzeltiyor, yoksa mı bozuyor? İkisi de doğru olabilir
**Önizleme satırı:** 324 kadın, multiple sklerozlu 65 hasta ve ergenlerde yapılan bir yeme penceresi çalışması — uyku ile diyet arasındaki ilişki hiç sanıldığı kadar basit değil.

Bu haftaki konu biraz kişisel bir merakdan çıktı. Keto ve aralıklı oruç yapan okuyucularımdan gelen mesajlar ikiye bölünmüş durumda: bir grup "hiç bu kadar dinlenmiş hissetmemiştim" diyor, diğer grup ilk haftalarda uykuya dalamadığından bahsediyor. İkisi de muhtemelen doğru söylüyor.

Araştırmaya başladığımda ilk bulduğum şey 2024'te European Psychiatry'de yayımlanan bir derleme oldu. Tunuslu bir ekip, 2012-2022 arası yayımlanmış 14 makaleyi tarayıp 8'ini incelemiş. Sonuç: diyetin melatonin üzerinden uykuyu etkileyebileceği düşünülüyor ama kendi ifadeleriyle bu "hâlâ tartışmalı" — mekanizma çalışmadan çalışmaya değişiyor.

Daha somut sayılar İtalya'dan geldi. 324 kadının 31 gün çok düşük kalorili bir ketojenik diyet uyguladığı bir çalışmada, iyi uyuduğunu söyleyenlerin oranı %42,9'dan %65,1'e çıkmış. Ama ilginç olan şu: regresyon analizinde bu iyileşmeyi açıklayan tek değişken kaybedilen yağ kütlesi oranıymış. Yani asıl faktör kilo kaybı, diyetin kendisi değil.

Multiple sklerozlu 65 hastada yapılan 6 aylık bir çalışma ise daha ayrıntılı bir tablo çiziyor: Epworth Uykululuk Skalası 1,9 puan düşmüş, uykusuzluk skorları 1,55 puan, uyku apnesi skorları 0,91 puan azalmış. Bu iyileşmelerin uyku süresinden bağımsız olduğu, azalan yorgunluk ve depresyonla ilişkili olduğu not edilmiş.

Madalyonun diğer yüzü de var. Keto'ya başladıktan sonraki ilk 1-2 hafta, karbonhidrat düşünce böbrekler sodyumu daha az tutuyor, suyla birlikte potasyum ve magnezyum da kayboluyor — bu da gece uyanmalarını kolaylaştırabiliyor. Aralıklı oruç tarafında da ilginç bir bulgu var: ergenlerde yapılan bir çalışmada 8 saatlik yeme penceresi uykuya zarar vermemiş, yetişkinlerde yapılan başka bir çalışmada ise pencere daraltıldığında yatmadan önce yemek yeme oranı %36'dan %14'e düşmüş.

## Medium

**Title:** What Keto Actually Does to Your Sleep, According to the Research
**Subtitle:** Two groups of people report opposite experiences — and it turns out the studies split the same way

Spend enough time in keto communities and you'll notice two contradictory stories running at once. Some people swear they've never slept better in their life. Others describe lying awake for hours in the first weeks, wired and restless despite being exhausted. Neither group is necessarily wrong, because when you actually dig into the research, it shows the same split.

The most cautious summary comes from a 2024 review in European Psychiatry. A Tunisian research team searched the literature on ketogenic diets and sleep disorders, found 14 papers published between 2012 and 2022, and included 8 of them. Their conclusion wasn't a clean yes or no — they suggest the diet may influence sleep through melatonin pathways, but call the effect "still controversed" because the proposed mechanisms don't line up consistently across studies.

The clearest numbers come from Italy. A 2023 study in the Journal of Translational Medicine retrospectively tracked 324 women with overweight or obesity through 31 days of a very low-calorie ketogenic diet, measuring sleep with the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. At the start, 42.9% reported sleeping well. By day 31, that number had climbed to 65.1%. But here's the catch: when researchers ran a regression to find out what actually predicted the improvement, the only significant factor was how much fat mass someone had lost. The diet's effect on sleep, in other words, appears to run mostly through weight loss rather than through anything specific to eating this way.

A more tightly controlled picture comes from an unexpected place: multiple sclerosis research. A study published in Sleep Medicine in October 2024 followed 65 people with relapsing MS for six months on a modified Atkins-style ketogenic diet, monitored daily with urine ketone testing. Eighty-seven percent completed the full six months. The results were notable across several measures at once — daytime sleepiness scores fell, the share of participants with excessive daytime sleepiness dropped from 15.4% to 7.7%, insomnia scores improved, and so did markers for sleep apnea and restless leg syndrome. The researchers noted these changes were independent of any shift in total sleep duration, and tracked alongside reductions in fatigue and depression. It's a strong signal, with the obvious caveat that it comes from a specific patient population, not healthy adults in general.

Then there's the other side of the story: the first couple of weeks. Cutting carbohydrates drops insulin, which makes the kidneys excrete more sodium, and water, potassium and magnesium go with it. That shift is a well-documented driver of "keto flu" symptoms generally, and it lines up neatly with reports of nighttime restlessness and trouble falling asleep early on. There isn't a rigorous study that puts hard numbers on this specific piece, but the mechanism is well established, and the practical fix — deliberate sodium and electrolyte intake — tends to help within days, not weeks.

If you're combining keto with intermittent fasting, the eating window itself raises a separate question. A 12-week trial in 50 adolescents with obesity found that an 8-hour eating window didn't hurt sleep quality or sleep efficiency compared with a longer window — a reassuring "no harm" finding, even if it fell short of a dramatic improvement once the statistics were adjusted. A different 12-week study in adults found something more specific: narrowing the eating window didn't change total sleep duration, but it cut the amount of late-night eating nearly in half, from 36% of nights down to 14%, which is itself a reasonable thing to want if you're trying to protect your sleep.

None of this adds up to a single rule. It adds up to a diet that can genuinely improve sleep for some people, mostly through weight loss and metabolic change, while also being capable of disrupting it for a few weeks through a well-understood and fixable electrolyte shift. Which version you get seems to depend less on willpower and more on whether you handle that adjustment period deliberately.
