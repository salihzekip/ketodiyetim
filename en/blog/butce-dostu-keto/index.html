Budget-Friendly Keto: 10 Ways to Do Keto Without Breaking the Bank

7 min read · Practical Guide · August 12, 2026

You may have heard "keto is expensive." True — avocado, organic meat, and specialty keto products can strain your wallet. But it's entirely possible to do keto on a budget. Here are 10 ways to do keto affordably in Turkey.

The core principle of keto isn't expensive products — it's choosing the right foods.

1. Make Eggs Your Staple

Eggs are Turkey's cheapest and most nutritious keto food. 1 egg = 6g protein, 5g fat, 0g carbs. Don't hesitate to eat 3–4 eggs a day.

2. Buy Seasonal Vegetables

Broccoli, cauliflower, zucchini, eggplant, peppers — these are both cheap and keto-friendly. Choose a seasonal greengrocer over the supermarket, and you'll save 40–50%.

3. Ground Beef and Cheaper Cuts

Instead of ribeye or lamb chops, go for ground beef, leg, or ribs. Fattier cuts already suit keto better — and they're usually cheaper too.

4. Canned Tuna

Rich in protein and omega-3, and very affordable. Choose the water-packed version and serve with olive oil.

5. Butter Instead of Sunflower Oil

If olive oil is expensive, butter is a great alternative. It has an excellent fat profile for keto and is also budget-friendly.

6. Buy in Bulk

Buy meat and cheese in larger quantities and freeze them. It's much cheaper per kilogram.

7. Make Keto Snacks at Home

Ready-made keto bars are very pricey. At home, boiled eggs, cheese, and walnuts serve the same purpose — cheaper and healthier.

8. Stay Away from Legumes

Lentils and chickpeas may look cheap, but they're high in carbs. Trying to save money this way can knock you out of ketosis.

9. Shop With a List

Unplanned shopping wrecks both your budget and your diet. Plan your weekly menu and buy exactly what you need.

10. You Don't Need "Keto" Branded Products

Products labeled "keto" are usually 3–5 times more expensive. Real foods (meat, eggs, vegetables, fat) are all you need for complete keto.

In Turkey, keto can comfortably be done on a budget of 150–250 TL per person per day.
