Can You Eat Fruit on Keto? Which Fruits Are Allowed?

5 min read · Nutrition Guide · August 12, 2026

One of the most common questions from keto beginners: "Can I eat fruit?" The answer is partly yes — but which fruit, and how much, matters a lot.

Fruit = natural sugar = carbs. Fruit choice matters a great deal on the keto diet.

❌ Fruits That Don't Fit Keto (High Sugar)

  • Banana: 1 medium = 27g of carbs. Strictly off-limits.
  • Grapes: 1 cup = 26g of carbs. Very high.
  • Mango: 1 cup = 25g of carbs. Off-limits.
  • Apple: 1 medium = 25g of carbs. That's your entire daily limit.
  • Oranges, tangerines, grapefruit: Relatively high in sugar.
  • Dried fruit: Dates, dried apricots, figs — sugar concentration is very high.

✅ Keto-Friendly Fruits (Low Sugar)

  • Avocado: Just 2g net carbs — keto's best friend. Rich in healthy fats.
  • Lemon/Lime: 1 piece = 4g net carbs. Add to your water and teas.
  • Strawberries: 100g = 6g net carbs. Fine as a small snack portion.
  • Raspberries: 100g = 5g net carbs. High fiber content keeps net carbs low.
  • Blackberries: 100g = 5g net carbs. Rich in antioxidants.
  • Watermelon: A small portion (80g) = 6g net carbs. Can be enjoyed in limited amounts.

What Is Net Carbs?

Net carbs = Total carbs - Fiber. Fiber isn't digested and doesn't affect your blood sugar. That's why high-fiber fruits can have lower net carbs than you'd expect.

You can spend 5–8g of your daily 20g net carb limit on fruit — but with careful portioning.
